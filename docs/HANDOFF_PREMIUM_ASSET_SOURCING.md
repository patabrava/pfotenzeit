# Pfotenzeit — professional asset sourcing and revised art direction

Prepared 2026-10-09 for Fable 5.1. Research only: no assets purchased, downloaded or integrated. Listing specifications and prices below are seller claims observed today, not measurements of delivered files. Visual judgments are Codex's assessment of public still previews; animation playback and real-phone performance remain unverified.

## User direction and decision

The user rejected the current character results and explicitly requested internet research for better models, with less emphasis on matching the generated sheets. This supersedes sheet fidelity as the visual acceptance criterion. Both Blender refinement passes remain prototype candidates and technical evidence, not approved production art.

Recommend **warm, believable animals in a softly art-directed school**. Natural puppy proportions, clear eyelids and gaze, a convincing muzzle, soft coat variation and controlled fur silhouettes should carry the experience. Keep adult humans and the courtyard visually consistent through material finish, light and palette. Preserve Nala's identity and the characters' roles; the old sheets are optional identity cues. Do not combine exaggerated cartoon eyes, photoreal hair, faceted bodies and tactical human outfits in the same scene.

**First integration candidate: Nyilonelycompany's Golden Retriever Puppy.** It fits Nala and has a useful Blender/glTF delivery path. **Best fluffy close-up benchmark in this review: PROmax3D's Maltipoo.** It is a different breed and considerably heavier; it should not silently redefine Nala. **Best practical ensemble candidate: RetroStyle Games' dog pack.** Evaluate it for visiting dogs after the hero passes. These are conditional sourcing recommendations, not purchase authorization or a claim that they are globally the best models.

## Acquisition requirements specific to this browser game

The current app serves open GLBs. A royalty-free label alone does not establish permission for that delivery method.

- [TurboSquid's published license guidance](https://www.turbosquid.com/de/licensing) requires advance approval for additional WebGL uses, with restrictions on open/extractable formats. Obtain written confirmation for Three.js and downloadable optimized GLB before buying A for this deployment.
- [PROmax3D terms](https://www.promax3d.com/pages/terms-of-service-license) require game files to be secured against extraction and restrict using assets/images in AI generation workflows. Its current terms are not a clear fit for public GLB delivery. Seek explicit browser and workflow permission before acquisition.
- [CGTrader guidance](https://help.cgtrader.com/hc/en-us/articles/360015124437-Royalty-Free-License) restricts standalone access and calls for measures preventing extraction. Clarify the selected seller/platform terms for C.
- The human pack's itch.io terms require compiled projects and prohibit sharing original files. Clarify browser distribution as well. For Fab alternatives, verify the selected license against the [Fab EULA](https://www.fab.com/eula) and any asset-specific restrictions.

Compression, renamed files or URL obfuscation do not establish license compliance. Keep paid source, textures and exported derivatives out of a public repository unless the license explicitly permits them.

Draft inquiry for the user to authorize or send (not sent):

> We are making a commercial mobile-first Three.js browser game. It loads modified, optimized GLB assets over HTTPS; users can technically retrieve these runtime files. We will not offer a standalone asset download or resell assets. Does your license explicitly permit this deployment? Can you provide written permission if needed? Please also confirm editable source, baked FBX/glTF animation delivery, the full clip list, eye/lid/jaw/ear controls, and whether local Blender scripting with an AI coding assistant is permitted without using the asset to train or generate an AI model.

## Fable execution order

1. Continue gameplay/UI/camera work independently. Preserve saves, German copy and simulation contracts. Do not regenerate live assets merely to match old sheets. Do not purchase, contact sellers or install dependencies without the necessary user authorization.
2. Resolve rights and obtain **one hero source**, not the entire shortlist. Keep provenance, receipt, selected license/version and any written permission. Stage under `art/vendor-evaluation/<vendor>/<asset>/`; retain originals outside public delivery.
3. Open through the existing Blender MCP connection in a separate scene. Inspect mesh, UVs, actual triangles, material count, texture memory, rig and all supplied clips. CAT/Unity/Unreal runtime systems do not transfer into Three.js; bake compatible skeletal clips. Preserve an editable `.blend` and reproducible conversion script.
4. Make a small actual Three.js hero review with neutral daylight and the intended courtyard lighting. Compare the original authored model and optimized candidate from identical front, side, portrait and gameplay cameras. No AI-generated image can stand in for a runtime screenshot.
5. Preserve stable asset IDs and semantic clip contracts. Keep the vendor rig if that preserves quality; build an explicit animation/attachment adapter or retarget deliberately. Do not assume compatibility with any 26-/32-bone procedural rig. Inspect Fable's current source before integration; this turn's live manifest still reports 26 joints and may lag parallel work.
6. Deliver all 13 required dog semantics: `idle_relaxed`, `look_at_handler`, `sniff`, `walk`, `sit`, `lie_down`, `rest_loop`, `eat`, `play_bow`, `turn_away`, `tense_idle`, `tired_idle`, `recover`. Author missing motions and validate their educational meaning. Tail wagging alone cannot label a dog relaxed. Do not substitute attack/death clips for stress/fatigue.
7. Build the growing variant as a believable anatomical change, not uniform scale. Fit the harness without intersections. Only after Nala's actual mobile slice is accepted should Bruno, the human cast and environment be adapted to the same finish.

## Shipping and acceptance

Use Game Studio's web-3D pipeline: Blender cleanup → GLB → existing glTF Transform tooling → validation → real runtime review. Preserve meters, +Y up, +Z local forward, floor pivot, normalized root and at most four skin influences. Avoid whole-model double-sided rendering as a blanket fix; inspect fur, eyelids and material sidedness independently.

Existing targets in `pfotenzeit/08_3D_ASSET_HANDOFF.md` remain: dog 12–18k triangles / ≤3 MB with clips, human 10–16k / ≤2 MB, initial compressed 3D ≤8 MB and later MVP ≤15 MB. Source counts above are not shipping results. Use selectively retained fur cards, sensible LODs and texture atlases; never simplify the muzzle/eyelids first. Meshopt/KTX2 are options only after loader/dependency compatibility is established; no dependency added in this research pass.

Visual acceptance: appealing face at gameplay size, readable gaze/ears/tail, natural joint bends, grounded paws, no plastic-looking coat, no visible fur-card bands or transparent sorting defects, stable highlights while moving, and coherent cast lighting. View at 360×640, 390×844 and 844×390 with the real HUD, plus 200% text and reduced motion. Include moving close-ups, not just a still render.

Verification after integration: `assets:check`, typecheck, relevant unit tests, build, Playwright smoke, headless WebGL and real-phone acceptance from `HANDOFF_VERIFICATION.md`. Record draw calls, visible triangles, texture memory, bytes, load time and sustained frame timing on named phones. Aim for stable ≥30 FPS as an evaluation target, not a measured promise. Preserve context-loss handling and fallback. Do not report vendor previews or software rendering as proof of phone performance.
